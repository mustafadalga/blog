In Turkey, a lot of things that are clearly illegal or dangerous have simply turned into normal cultural habits. Firing guns into the air at weddings, running red lights, parking cars right on the sidewalk and blocking pedestrians, smoking inside non-smoking places, or throwing trash into the sea don't even shock people anymore.

People break these rules easily just for five minutes of comfort or fun. Someone parks on the sidewalk saying "I will only be two minutes" while pedestrians are forced to walk in traffic. People shoot guns at weddings to celebrate and put everyone around them in danger. Because most of society tolerates this behavior, nobody really speaks up against it.

The reason this keeps happening isn't a lack of laws. On paper, every single one of these things is already illegal. The real issue is that breaking the rules became socially acceptable, and the government looks the other way.

Politicians and local officials avoid strictly enforcing these laws or giving out real fines. If you punish something that almost everyone does, voters get angry at you. Especially before elections, politicians ignore these violations so they don't lose votes. Regular people don't want to follow the rules, and politicians don't want to lose popularity.

So when society treats breaking the law as normal culture and the government ignores it for votes, writing new laws doesn't do anything. The rules just stay on paper while everything stays the same.
