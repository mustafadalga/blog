Whenever things go badly in a country, when the economy crashes or a refugee crisis gets worse, one of the oldest tricks politicians use is blaming "foreign enemies." Machiavellian leaders use this tactic really well because it is the easiest way to hide their own failures and corruption.

When the currency loses value or food prices jump up in the supermarket, they immediately blame America, Europe, or some mysterious outside forces. That way they redirect people's anger away from their own bad policies and broken management toward an imaginary enemy outside the borders. For a lot of people it is easier to believe in foreign conspiracies than to look at how badly their own country is run.

Some people also think that simply voting out one government and bringing in another will magically fix the economy overnight. But swapping names doesn't solve anything on its own. If the economic system, resource management, and core policies stay broken, the next group runs into the exact same wall.

This isn't something unique to Turkey either, leaders all over the world play the exact same game. Whenever there is a big mess inside the country, they invent an outside threat to distract everyone. While people are busy getting mad at foreign powers, nobody asks where the tax money went or why prices keep rising.

Nothing actually gets fixed this way. Blaming imaginary enemies outside just buys time for politicians to keep their seats, while the real internal problems keep getting worse.
