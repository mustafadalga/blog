In Turkey, everybody constantly complains about corruption, unfairness, and bad morals. But if you watch people closely, you notice a simple truth: most people don't actually care about justice. They only complain because the corrupt system isn't working for them.

If they had the chance to steal, skip the line, or use connections, they would do it immediately without feeling guilty at all.

Take the hospital as an everyday example. Everyone gets furious when someone walks past a 50-person queue into the doctor's office because they know someone on the inside. But if that same angry person had a cousin working at that hospital, they would call them in a second to skip the line. They don't hate line-skipping. They just hate having to wait.

It is the exact same thing with government jobs and nepotism. People act disgusted when politicians give high-paying jobs to their family members. But the moment those same angry people get even a tiny bit of power or authority, the first thing they do is try to place their own brother, friend, or son into a job. The problem was never that favoritism is bad; the problem was that their own family didn't get a piece of the cake.

You see the same hypocrisy with the economy and refugees. People complain loudly that migrants are working illegally and ruining the job market. But the exact same people will hire an undocumented migrant to paint their house, build their fence, or work in their factory for half the minimum wage to save money. They love the cheap labor when it benefits their own pocket.

Look at how people treat public property. In school, I saw students break desks, kick doors, and trash equipment for fun. People don't care because "it belongs to the government, not to me."

People don't want an honest system where the rules apply equally to everyone. They just want their own turn to enjoy the unfair privileges.
