In real life, justice is never 100% fair. The entire system is designed and run by flawed humans, which makes complete justice practically impossible.

To start with, the people making the decisions (judges, prosecutors, police officers, and juries) are just regular human beings. They carry their own personal biases, prejudices, and blind spots into the courtroom. Social discrimination based on race, gender, religion, or background does not disappear when someone puts on a judge's robe. A bad mood, a personal bias, or a simple clerical error can completely change a verdict and ruin someone's life.

Then there is the reality of money. In any legal dispute, wealth changes the outcome. Rich individuals and corporations can afford teams of top lawyers, private investigators, and years of endless appeals until the other side runs out of cash. If you are poor, you are stuck with an overworked public defender who barely has time to review your case. The legal system itself is packed with confusing jargon and heavy bureaucracy, making it almost impossible for an ordinary citizen to defend themselves without spending a fortune.

Politics makes the situation worse. Governments and powerful politicians regularly interfere with courts to protect their own interests. In authoritarian countries, the justice system is not independent at all; it functions as a weapon to punish critics and protect the state.

Even when people try to play by the book, the laws themselves have problems. Because laws are written by politicians, they are full of loopholes. Smart lawyers know how to exploit these technicalities to get guilty clients off the hook. Many laws are also decades old and do not fit modern reality.

Access is another barrier. If you live in a rural area far from major cities, or if you do not speak the country's official language well, going through the legal process is an uphill battle. As legal services move online, people without computers or internet skills get left behind even more. Most people do not even know their basic constitutional rights, making it easy for employers, landlords, or police to take advantage of them.

Finally, a massive amount of injustice never even reaches a courtroom. For sensitive issues like domestic violence or sexual assault, victims often stay silent because they fear revenge, social shame, or a police force that will not take them seriously. If a crime is never reported, the law cannot do anything about it.

Courts can try to be more transparent and accessible, but as long as human beings run the machinery, perfect justice will remain an ideal on paper rather than reality.
