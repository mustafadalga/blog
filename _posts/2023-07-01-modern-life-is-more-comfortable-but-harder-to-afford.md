From the outside modern life looks very comfortable. Technology improved, phones are fast, and there are a lot of new career options. But when you look at people's daily lives, things aren't actually easier than before, in fact they feel more stressful and complicated.

A few generations ago life was much simpler. Options like going to university or becoming a doctor were limited, and most people did physical labor. Technology wasn't advanced, so expectations and daily routines weren't this tangled up either.

Today technology changed a lot of that. For example as a software developer you can work remotely from home. That sounds great on paper, except it brings a different kind of stress: you have to stay connected all the time, and the line between work and personal life basically disappears. Even late at night you are still checking messages or thinking about work.

Then there is the economic side of it, which is the biggest trap. Looking at the numbers, it seems like people earn more money today. But inflation and living costs went up so fast that even when your salary grows, you actually buy less with it. We earn bigger numbers on paper, yet we can afford fewer things in real life.

Basic life milestones like buying a house, owning a car, or going on a vacation have turned into stressful goals that feel out of reach for most normal workers. People work the whole month just to pay rent and bills, which creates constant anxiety.

Technology made things faster and reduced physical labor, sure, but it didn't automatically make life better or calmer. We got more comfort and screens, while actually affording a normal life got much harder than it used to be.
