People love talking about copying successful systems from other countries, and Finland's education model is always the top example. But you can't just copy and paste a system from one country into another with completely different conditions. There are pretty obvious reasons why Finland's system wouldn't work in Turkey.

The first big issue is teachers and constant political changes. In Finland, teaching is a very respected and competitive job, and teachers actually have the freedom to decide how to teach their classes. Their education policies are long term, so the system doesn't flip upside down every time there is an election. In Turkey, teachers deal with low salaries, economic stress, and a strict central curriculum with zero flexibility. On top of that every new minister or government changes the exams and the rules all over again.

Then there is the economy and brain drain. Finland puts real money and solid resources into schools. In Turkey, a lot of schools lack basic materials and proper funding. Worse than that, the smartest and best trained people who could actually improve things keep leaving Turkey for better living conditions and salaries abroad.

There is also the problem of focusing on quantity instead of quality. Opening new universities in every city made the number of university students look bigger on paper, but overall education quality dropped badly because of it.

So trying to copy Finland's model into a country with an unstable economy, constantly changing political rules, and ongoing brain drain simply doesn't work.
