In theory elections are supposed to let people choose leaders they actually believe in, but in real life it rarely works like that. When voters go to the ballot box, most of the time they don't pick someone they genuinely like, they just feel forced to vote for the lesser of two evils.

Seeing new faces in politics feels almost impossible. The exact same leaders sit in the same seats for decades whether they win or lose elections. Staying in that chair has nothing to do with being a great leader, it is all about internal party politics and protecting their own interests so nobody new can replace them.

Even when a politician looks popular, it doesn't always mean they are doing a good job. A lot of the time they only look good because their rivals are even worse or more incompetent. So voters end up picking whoever seems slightly less terrible.

On top of that there is blind party loyalty. Many people stick with the same party for years, and because nothing ever changes inside that party, they keep voting for the exact same names over and over again because they feel like there is no other option.

Both the parties and the voters have basically accepted this. Instead of choosing good leaders, democracy just turns into picking the least bad option on the list.
