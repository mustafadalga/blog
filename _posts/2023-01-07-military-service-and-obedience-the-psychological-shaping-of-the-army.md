Many things that happen in the military look nonsensical and stupid from the outside.

In the first days, they repeat the same phrases all the time: "A soldier doesn't sleep," "A soldier doesn't get tired," "A soldier doesn't get hungry," "A soldier doesn't get sick," and "A soldier doesn't get cold." It sounds like they treat people like robots, but the main goal is to push your physical and mental limits.

Some situations are really meaningless. For example, not being allowed to turn on the bathroom light when a commander walks by, apologizing to a rifle, or punishing a tank. There is only one reason for this: to force you to obey rules and orders, no matter how stupid they are.

Breakfast inspections are the same. Soldiers pretend there is still food on their plates. The goal here is not logic, but getting people used to the chain of command.

Edward Snowden talks about this in his book "Permanent Record." The army's goal is to erase your individuality so you obey. They make soldiers tired both physically and mentally on purpose. When you are completely exhausted, you cannot question anything. You just follow orders without thinking. That is the kind of soldier the army wants: someone who does not question and simply obeys.

If you are educated and have worked on yourself, these things feel completely useless and annoying. But for young people around 20 to 24 with little life experience, maybe it teaches them how to stay strong against hard situations in life.
