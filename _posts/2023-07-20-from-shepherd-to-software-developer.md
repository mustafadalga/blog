Life does not start equally for everyone. Some people are born into good conditions, and others have to deal with hard realities when they are still kids. I grew up in a place with very limited opportunities because of where I was born and my family's situation.

When I was just six years old, my job was to take our goats and sheep up to the mountains to graze. Every morning before the sun came up I took them out, and in the afternoon when the weather cooled down a bit I went back out again. At home there was no internet and no proper place to sit and study.

![]({{ '/assets/images/8aa708c3-6f06-4e78-a09e-0af0c0a0f940.jpeg' | relative_url }})

In high school I studied computer science, even though I didn't own a computer and had zero internet access at home. Still I tried to learn Visual Basic, Python, and C#. My family and the people around me didn't really get what I was doing, they just thought I was wasting time staring at a screen.

![]({{ '/assets/images/9c80e495-7185-4191-bd0f-00c0a7c5fe93.jpeg' | relative_url }})

One time I heard about a free Linux summer camp and wanted to go, but I couldn't afford it so I missed out. Still I kept going. Getting into university felt almost impossible at first, but I managed to do it. After university I started learning English, which also felt really hard back then.

When I see successful developers like Emma Bostian, I think about how much easier their starting conditions were. Maybe her life was easier and maybe I had to work harder for the same things.

Where you are born and what family you come from definitely affect your life, there is no point pretending it doesn't. But complaining about it doesn't change anything either, you just work with what you have and keep moving.
