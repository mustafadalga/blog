Many people assume equality means giving everybody the exact same thing. But in daily life, treating everyone identically is often unfair. There is a simple distinction between equality and fairness: equality gives everybody the exact same pair of shoes, while fairness gives each person shoes that actually fit their feet.

Work and effort are never identical between two people. In any job, two workers can have the same title, but one stays focused for ten hours while the other does the bare minimum and leaves early. When both receive the exact same pay in the name of equality, the person working harder feels cheated. People also learn at different speeds and bring different skills to a team. If high performers and low performers get treated the exact same way, the most capable people lose their motivation to put in extra effort.

Resources and money are also limited. If public assistance is divided equally among all citizens, a wealthy person receives the same financial support as someone who cannot afford food. The same applies to healthcare: giving a healthy person and a sick person the exact same treatment is equal on paper, but it does not solve the actual problem. Real fairness requires directing resources where they are needed rather than handing out identical portions.

Enforcing strict equality also requires taking away individual freedom. If someone decides to work on weekends, learn new skills, or build a side business to earn more money, the only way to keep them equal to everyone else is to stop them or take their extra income away. You cannot force identical outcomes across a society without controlling what people are allowed to do with their time.

Because human beings have different goals, work ethics, and needs, forcing everyone to end up in the exact same position does not work in practice. Fair opportunities make sense, but forced equal results do not.
