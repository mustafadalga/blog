Almost everybody agrees that lying is wrong, yet almost everybody lies from time to time.

Harold Perrineau’s character in the OZ series sums this up simply:

> “Most people will tell you lying is wrong and then immediately go out and lie like a motherfucker. We all know lies are necessary. They also say if you repeat a lie often enough, it becomes the truth. Or at least, you convince yourself it is the fucking gospel.”

Most of the time, people lie simply because it is the easiest option. When the truth is messy, painful, or annoying, telling a lie saves time and helps you skip an uncomfortable conversation.

People also lie to protect themselves. If telling the truth means getting punished, losing a job, getting yelled at, or being rejected by people around you, hiding the truth works like basic self-defense.

A lot of daily lying happens just to avoid constant fights with other people. Telling a friend you are busy instead of saying you don't want to see them, or saying something looks good when it doesn't, keeps daily social life running without unnecessary drama.

People also lie to control how others see them. They exaggerate stories, hide embarrassing details, or pretend to be doing better than they actually are just to maintain a certain image.

Behind almost all of this is fear. People fear failure, criticism, looking weak, or being rejected by the group. Whenever telling the truth feels unsafe, lying becomes an automatic habit.
