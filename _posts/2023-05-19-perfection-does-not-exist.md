Perfection does not exist. First of all, it does not have one single definition. Something that looks completely right to one person can look ordinary or unfinished to someone else or in another culture. Because there is no fixed standard that everyone agrees on, it is impossible to decide what is truly perfect.

Also, conditions change all the time. A piece of software, a design, or an idea that looks complete today becomes old and not good enough in just a few years as technology and expectations change. When standards shift every day, a fixed point of perfection cannot exist.

There is also a simple reality about how people learn: we learn by making mistakes. The moment you accept that something is completely perfect and finished, you stop learning and stop improving it. In real life, there is always something new to learn, a bug to fix, or a detail you can make better. Mistakes are a normal part of any process, and expecting perfection blocks that.

The pressure to be perfect only creates unnecessary stress and a fear of failing. Someone who waits for everything to be completely right often struggles to even start. When you get stuck trying to polish one small detail, time passes and you lose the chance to try other things or experience life.

In short, perfection is not something that exists in real life. It is just an unreachable expectation that people build inside their own heads.
