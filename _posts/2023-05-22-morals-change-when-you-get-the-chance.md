Most people try to look very moral and principled on the outside. But in real life things don't work like that at all. All those big moral rules people defend disappear the second they clash with their own personal interests.

Actually there isn't really a strong moral code here, most of the time people just don't have the opportunity yet. A lot of people strongly criticize certain things and call them immoral, but the minute they get the chance to do the exact same thing, they do it without thinking twice.

Look at taxes as a simple example. Everybody gets angry and swears at big companies for avoiding taxes. But when that exact same person opens a small shop or gets paid in cash, the first thing they do is not give a receipt and avoid paying taxes themselves. When a big company does it, it's theft. When they do it, they just say "the government already takes too much" and move on.

Another example is men who act like moral police about how women dress. On the street or online there are always guys complaining about women wearing revealing clothes, calling it immoral or shameful. But if one of those same women gave those guys even a tiny bit of attention, they would run to be with her immediately. Their problem isn't really about morals, they just don't have access to it.

It is the same with people getting angry at someone using connections to skip a hospital line. Most of them aren't mad because skipping the line is unfair. They are mad because they don't know anyone working inside that hospital. If their own cousin worked there, they would skip the 50 person queue without feeling any shame.

Most of the time people seem honest not because they actually are honest, but because they haven't found the power or the chance to cheat yet. Once the opportunity shows up at their door, all those rules they defended go straight out the window.
