In Turkey, the minimum wage has been raised over and over again in recent years. At first glance it looks like a good thing because workers get bigger numbers on their paychecks, but in reality it doesn't work out that way. Instead of actually increasing purchasing power, these raises just create an endless loop.

The moment the minimum wage goes up, labor costs immediately jump for businesses across all sectors, especially big supermarket chains like A101, BİM, and Şok that employ huge numbers of minimum wage workers. Businesses never absorb that extra cost themselves, they just add it straight to the price of the products they sell. Before the worker even gets their new paycheck, milk, bread, and groceries are already more expensive.

Then there is everybody else who was earning slightly above minimum wage. When prices jump in every store and their salaries don't go up at the same rate, their purchasing power drops right away too. Suddenly people with skilled jobs feel like they are back down at minimum wage level.

After that the whole cycle starts over again. Because everything in the stores got more expensive, people cannot afford to live and start demanding higher wages again. When wages go up again, companies raise prices again to cover their costs.

So on paper your salary looks bigger, but in real life you can buy the exact same amount of stuff, or even less. Constantly raising the minimum wage doesn't fix a broken economy, it only pushes inflation higher.
