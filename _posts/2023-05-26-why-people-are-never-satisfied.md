Even when we have enough to live comfortably, humans always want more. We constantly chase something bigger, better, or newer. There are pretty simple biological and social reasons behind why people are never satisfied.

A lot of it comes from evolution. In the past, surviving and passing on your genes meant constantly looking for more food, a safer shelter, and better resources. The people who settled and stopped looking didn't survive as easily. Our brains are still wired that way. Even when our basic survival needs are met, we still push for more security and higher social status.

Society and culture also push this even harder. Everything around us tells people to buy more stuff, chase more money, and climb up in status. A lot of the time people want new things just so they don't fall behind everybody else.

There is also the problem of getting used to things too fast, which psychologists call the hedonic treadmill. When you buy a new phone, get a new job, or start a new relationship, you feel really happy at first. But after a short time that excitement fades away and it just feels normal. Soon you feel unsatisfied again and start looking for the next thing.

This endless dissatisfaction has both a good and a bad side. On one side, it pushes humans to invent new things, build better technology, and keep improving. On the other side, it creates constant stress and makes people feel like nothing they ever get is enough. You get what you wanted, and then immediately want the next thing.
